# Syllabus

The purpose of CS 501 is to prepare the Computer Science TAs for teaching in the classroom environment. TAs are not expected to have any prior teaching experience, so this course will help them achieve confidence in the classroom, grade equitably and efficiently, teach to various learning styles and establish their personal teaching philosophies. The course is given to both students who will be TAing concurrently in the fall quarter and those who hope to TA in the future.

## Requirements

Your time as a graduate student is precious and limited. Thus, you'll only need to complete the following list of requirements to pass the course. For the entire fall quarter, please expect to spend a total of 5 hours in class, and around 5-8 hours after class. Spread across the quarter, the workload comes out to be just above **1 hour per week** in combined in-class and out-of-class time, which I hope is manageable.

| Who      | What you need to do                                                                             | Estimated time                                       |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Everyone | Attend classes from week 1 - 5                                                                  | 5 hours (in class)                                   |
| Everyone | Complete the autograder lab                                                                     | N/A: we'll do this in class                          |
| Everyone | Observe a discussion/lab section led by a fellow TA                                             | 1 hour                                               |
| Everyone | Go to the class on drafting a teaching philosophy statement                                     | 1 hour (in class)                                    |
| Everyone | Write a teaching philosophy statement                                                           | 1 hour                                               |
| TAs      | Maintain a teaching journal                                                                     | 5-10 minutes per week                                |
| Non-TAs  | Give a 10-min teaching demo                                                                     | Prep time + sign up for 1 class where you'll present |


## Attendance
Attendance is **mandatory from week 1 through week 6**, and will be taken in class. 

From week 7 to week 10, you'll only need to attend exactly 1 week of class:
- If you're a non-TA, sign up for 1 class to give your micro teaching demo.
- If you're a TA, sign up for 1 class to attend and give feedback.

## Schedule
**note:** This section will likely not appear in the syllabus. I am using this primarily as a place to draft a tentative plan.

### Week 1 - Course overview and icebreaker
**Objectives:**
- Students will know what to expect for the rest of the course, including what is required of them to pass and how much time each requirement should take.
- Students will be (somewhat) comfortable sharing their peers.
- Students will feel confident in their ability to plan their first section.

**Activities:**
- Course overview slides (mostly recycled from prior year)
- Icebreaker think/pair/share: Have students consider one thing that is daunting about TAing; pair up to brainstorm what they can do to make TAing less daunting, and what they would like to get out of the TA training class; then discuss class wide
- Helpful tips for week 1

### Week 2 - Designing objectives and lesson plans
**Objectives:**
- Students will be able to write learning objectives
- Students will be familiar with some active learning strategies
- Students will begin to develop/be aware of tools to reflect on and analyze their own teaching

**Activities:**
- Students will individually write a learning objective for something they could see themselves teaching as a TA. Later students will form groups to share and revise their objectives
- Mix of slides and classwide discussion of what makes engaging objectives/learning strategies; why they are important (can mostly recycle from previous years)

### Week 3 - Efficient grading
**Objectives:**
- Students will be aware of techniques to improve their grading efficiency/fairness

**Activities:**
- Students will individually assess a couple of mock question/answer pairs. After we discuss strategies as a class, students we'll repeat and share what was helpful/not helpful
- Optional: autograder lab - there seems to be a well structured activity from last year on designing/breaking autograders; I've never used these, so I a) don't think I will be of much help, and b) don't think it should be mandatory for everyone

### Week 4 - How to support students and yourself
**Objectives:**
- Students will know that their are campus resources they can direct their students to, and take advantage of themselves

**Activities:**
- This can be taken essentially as is from previous year; already includes roleplaying and group scenarios for dealing with various issues students might face, as well as informational slides about campus resources. I will go through the LTAI resources we learned and fill in any gaps

### Week 5 - How to craft teaching philosophy statement ###
**Objectives:**
- Students will be able to articulate their teaching style/why they teach that way, how they've implemented their teaching style (evidence), and how they assess their success, i.e. write a statement draft

**Activities:**
- Sharing teaching journals/brainstorming ideas in group (ideally, this will have happened in earlier weeks too, maybe every other week?)
- Short slidedeck
