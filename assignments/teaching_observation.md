# Teaching Observation

**Who:** Everyone

**Deadline:** Week 10 of Fall quarter

**Estimated Time:** 1.5 hours

## What you need to do

1. Please pick one TA's section to observe from [this sign up sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1wpXJCAOXG35N3z6-BThvrmAO1QHWKV4UrOyT8Vx4ko8/edit?gid=0#gid=0). You are free to pick any TA you want, and any section that fits your schedule, but please try to ensure that each TA gets at least one signup. If I were you, I would pick a section that I could see myself teaching in the future!
2. Contact the TA well in advance (via email/in person) to let them know that you'd like to observe their section, and **confirm the day/time/location with them**. Don't just show up unannounced! This is both to let the TA know that you'll be coming, and to ensure you show up **on time** at the right place when the TA is teaching (be aware that not every TA teaches every week!)
3. Put your name on the sign-up sheet next to the TA's section that you're observing. 
4. Attend the section, and use [this TA observation form](https://docs.google.com/document/d/14hyysr2nPwQHWHIm_jI6JR4pu8awx0g14399sLeZInc/edit?tab=t.0) to provide **constructive feedback** to the TA on their teaching. Please be kind and respectful in your feedback. Put yourself in their shoes: what kind of feedback would you want to receive if you were the TA?
5. After the section, please email a copy of your filled-out observation form to the TA, and **cc Thomas** (tschibler@ucsb.edu) so that he can record your completion of this task.

## What I hope you get out of it

1. If you aren't a TA yet, this is a great opportunity to see what a real section looks like, and adjust your expectations!
2. If you are a TA, you should (hopefully) get some great constructive feedback from your peers to improve your teaching in a low stakes setting.
3. Regardless of your TA status, you'll get to see someone else's teaching style, which can be a great time to reflect on your own teaching beliefs. What did you like? What didn't you like? What might you incorporate into your own teaching? (You're welcome to add these thoughts to your teaching journal if you are a TA!)
